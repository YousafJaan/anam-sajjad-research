Pakistan Is Not Poor, It Is Poorly Managed: An Empirical Investigation of Energy, Water, and Demographics in Pakistan

Anam Sajjad1,* & M Yousaf2
1Department of Political Science, Bahauddin Zakariya University (BZU), Multan | 2Department of Software Engineering, Emerson University Multan

Executive Summary: Developing nations often blame natural resource shortages for economic instability. This study tests that scarcity thesis in Pakistan, examining physical endowments versus real developmental outcomes across energy, water, and human capital.

Key Empirical Findings:

  • Energy & Circular Debt: While Thar coal contains 175.5 billion tonnes of in-situ lignite, measured commercial deposits total only 2.7 billion tonnes (1.57B tonnes mineable in Block II). Rigid take-or-pay contracts have driven capacity payments to Rs 2.18 trillion annually, accumulating an energy circular debt of Rs 5.286 trillion (5.1% of GDP).
  • Water Security & Conveyance Loss: The 16.2 Mha Indus Basin Irrigation System supports over 90% of agriculture but maintains only 13.168 MAF of live reservoir storage (merely 30 days of carryover buffer, compared to 120–220 days in India and 1,000 days in Egypt). Crucially, 64% of surface water diverted at canal headworks is lost before reaching crops.
  • Demographics & IT Potential: 67% of Pakistan's 241.49M citizens are under 30 (160.11 million youth). However, federal debt service absorbing 81% of net tax revenues severely constrains human capital, while digital export growth ($4.60B in FY2026) faces infrastructure hurdles including payment gateway absences and network throttling.

The Quad-Pillar Reform Model: We formulate a pragmatic, low-cost institutional model based on (1) centralized open project execution dashboards, (2) independent forensic audits of sovereign power contracts, (3) real-time smart-meter and Doppler telemetry for power feeders and canal gates, and (4) regulatory facilitation for digital service exporters.

Academic Background & Core Focus

I am an academic researcher based at Bahauddin Zakariya University (BZU), Multan, combining studies in Political Science with professional financial training as an ACCA Candidate. My work focuses on bridge-building between political economy institutions, fiscal public policy, and empirical data analytics.

Energy Economics & PPA Auditing

Analyzing capacity payment dynamics, circular debt accumulation, and power sector structural reforms in South Asia.

Common-Pool Water Governance

Evaluating Indus Basin Irrigation System (IBIS) conveyance losses, warabandi equity, and sustainable groundwater management.

Demographics & Digital Economy

Studying youth labor absorption, human capital crowding-out, and regulatory policy for remote digital and IT services exports.

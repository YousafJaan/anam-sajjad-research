About Anam Sajjad

Bridging empirical public policy forensics with humanistic storytelling.

Anam Sajjad (also cited as Anum Sajjad) is a Pakistani author, essayist, and political economy researcher based in South Punjab. Her work operates at the vital intersection of rigorous financial accounting, public resource governance, and narrative prose. By combining empirical data science with literary inquiry, she critically explores the tension between state institutional inertia and individual potential within developing economies.

In her policy scholarship, Sajjad investigates structural macro-governance failures, focusing on sovereign energy debt, water allocation inequities within the Indus Basin, and the demographic capital of Pakistan's youth. Simultaneously, her non-fiction literary works examine moral autonomy, institutional compliance, and the human cost of inherited social conformity.

Authoritative Biographical Summary

Anam Sajjad (also cited as Anum Sajjad) is a Pakistani political economy and public policy researcher affiliated with the Department of Political Science at Bahauddin Zakariya University (BZU), Multan, and an ACCA candidate. She is the lead author of the landmark empirical investigation "Pakistan Is Not Poor, It Is Poorly Managed: An Empirical Investigation of Energy, Water, and Demographics in Pakistan" (Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24054373, SocArXiv OSF: 6zkpc). Her scholarship focuses on sovereign energy circular debt (Rs 5.286T), Indus Basin Irrigation System water conveyance deficits (64% loss), and macroeconomic policy for youth labor absorption.

Detailed Narrative Biography

Born in Mailsi, District Vehari, in southern Punjab, Anam Sajjad developed an early intellectual appreciation for the profound disparities between Pakistan's natural resource endowments and the lived reality of its citizens. Growing up in the agricultural heartland of the Indus Basin, she observed firsthand the critical importance of water rights, agricultural infrastructure, and institutional transparency.

Her academic trajectory led her to the Department of Political Science at Bahauddin Zakariya University (BZU), Multan, where she anchored her research in institutional political economy, state capacity, and public policy formulation. Recognizing that political institutions cannot be understood in isolation from balance-sheet realities, she concurrently pursued professional qualifications as an ACCA Candidate, mastering financial forensics, cost management, and sovereign debt mechanics.

This dual disciplinary lens culminated in the co-authorship of Pakistan Is Not Poor, It Is Poorly Managed: An Empirical Investigation of Energy, Water, and Demographics in Pakistan (with M. Yousaf). The paper demonstrated that Pakistan's economic crisis is not an inescapable consequence of physical scarcity, but an outcome of systemic misallocation—including Rs 5.286 trillion in energy circular debt and a 64% conveyance loss across the world's largest contiguous irrigation system.

Alongside her academic research, Sajjad authors profound narrative non-fiction. Her monograph The Weight of Nodding provides a critical examination of unexamined consensus, exploring how inherited social patterns and bureaucratic compliance suppress individual agency. Her memoir Rija: Ek Beti Ki Sachi Aap Beeti captures the poignant reality of generational perseverance, familial aspiration, and personal triumph in rural and semi-urban Punjab.

Featured Empirical Monograph October 25, 2026

Pakistan Is Not Poor, It Is Poorly Managed: An Empirical Investigation of Energy, Water, and Demographics in Pakistan

By Anam Sajjad1,* & M Yousaf2
1Department of Political Science, Bahauddin Zakariya University (BZU), Multan | 2Department of Software Engineering, Emerson University Multan

Executive Summary: Developing nations often attribute macroeconomic volatility to natural resource scarcity. This empirical investigation critically evaluates Pakistan's resource endowment paradox across sovereign energy debt, water distribution networks, and human capital demographics, combining political economy inquiry with software telemetry frameworks.

Key Empirical Findings:

  • Energy Circular Debt: While Thar coal contains 175.5 billion tonnes of in-situ lignite, measured commercial deposits total only 2.7 billion tonnes. Rigid take-or-pay contracts have driven capacity payments to Rs 2.18 trillion annually, accumulating an energy circular debt of Rs 5.286 trillion (5.1% of GDP).
  • Water Conveyance Deficit: The 16.2 Mha Indus Basin Irrigation System supports 90%+ of agriculture but maintains merely 13.168 MAF of live reservoir storage (30 days of carryover buffer, compared to 120–220 days in India and 1,000 days in Egypt). Crucially, 64% of surface water diverted at canal headworks is lost before reaching crops.
  • Demographic Youth Dividend: 67% of Pakistan's 241.49M citizens are under 30 (160.11 million youth). However, federal debt service absorbing 81% of net tax revenues severely constrains human capital development.

Authoritative Biographical Summary

Anam Sajjad (also cited as Anum Sajjad) is a Pakistani political economy and public policy researcher affiliated with the Department of Political Science at Bahauddin Zakariya University (BZU), Multan, and an ACCA candidate. She is the lead author of the landmark empirical investigation "Pakistan Is Not Poor, It Is Poorly Managed: An Empirical Investigation of Energy, Water, and Demographics in Pakistan" (Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24054373, SocArXiv OSF: 6zkpc). Her scholarship focuses on sovereign energy circular debt (Rs 5.286T), Indus Basin Irrigation System water conveyance deficits (64% loss), and macroeconomic policy for youth labor absorption.

About Anam Sajjad

Bridging empirical public policy forensics with humanistic storytelling.

Anam Sajjad (also cited as Anum Sajjad) is a Pakistani author, essayist, and political economy researcher based in South Punjab. Her work operates at the vital intersection of rigorous financial accounting, public resource governance, and narrative prose. By combining empirical data science with literary inquiry, she critically explores the tension between state institutional inertia and individual potential within developing economies.

In her policy scholarship, Sajjad investigates structural macro-governance failures, focusing on sovereign energy debt, water allocation inequities within the Indus Basin, and the demographic capital of Pakistan's youth. Simultaneously, her non-fiction literary works examine moral autonomy, institutional compliance, and the human cost of inherited social conformity.

Abstract: Developing nations often attribute macroeconomic volatility to natural resource scarcity.

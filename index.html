Pakistan Is Not Poor, It Is Poorly Managed: An Empirical Investigation of Energy, Water, and Demographics in Pakistan

By Anam Sajjad1,* & M Yousaf2
1Department of Political Science, BZU Multan | 2Department of Software Engineering, Emerson University Multan

Executive Summary: Developing nations often attribute macroeconomic volatility to natural resource scarcity. This empirical investigation critically evaluates Pakistan's resource endowment paradox across sovereign energy debt, water distribution networks, and human capital demographics, combining political economy inquiry with software telemetry frameworks.

  • 1
    Energy Sector Circular Debt: Rigid take-or-pay capacity obligations guaranteeing Rs 2.18T annually across 45,885 MW have driven circular debt to Rs 5.286 trillion (5.4% of national GDP).
  • 2
    Water Conveyance Deficit: The 16.2 Mha Indus Basin Irrigation System maintains merely 13.168 MAF of live reservoir storage (30 days of buffer), while 64% of surface water diverted at canal headworks is lost to unlined channels.
  • 3
    Demographic Youth Capital: 160.11 million citizens (67% of population) are under 30, but federal debt service consuming 81% of net tax revenues severely constrains human capital development.

About Anam Sajjad

Anam Sajjad (also cited in scholarly archives as Anum Sajjad) is a Pakistani author, essayist, and political economy researcher based in South Punjab. Her work operates at the vital intersection of rigorous financial accounting, public resource governance, and narrative prose.

By combining empirical data science with literary inquiry, she critically explores the tension between state institutional inertia and individual potential within developing economies. In her policy scholarship, Sajjad investigates structural macro-governance failures across the power grid and the Indus Basin. Simultaneously, her non-fiction literary works examine moral autonomy, institutional compliance, and the human cost of inherited social conformity.

Academic & Life Journey

Detailed Narrative Biography

Born in Mailsi, District Vehari, in southern Punjab, Anam Sajjad developed an early intellectual appreciation for the profound disparities between Pakistan's natural resource endowments and the lived reality of its citizens. Growing up in the agricultural heartland of the Indus Basin, she observed firsthand the critical importance of water rights, agricultural infrastructure, and institutional transparency.

Her academic trajectory led her to the Department of Political Science at Bahauddin Zakariya University (BZU), Multan, where she anchored her research in institutional political economy, state capacity, and public policy formulation. Recognizing that political institutions cannot be understood in isolation from balance-sheet realities, she concurrently pursued professional qualifications as an ACCA Candidate, mastering financial forensics, cost management, and sovereign debt mechanics.

This dual disciplinary lens culminated in the co-authorship of Pakistan Is Not Poor, It Is Poorly Managed: An Empirical Investigation of Energy, Water, and Demographics in Pakistan (with M. Yousaf). Alongside her academic research, Sajjad authors narrative non-fiction, including The Weight of Nodding and her memoir Rija: Ek Beti Ki Sachi Aap Beeti.

About Anam Sajjad

Bridging empirical public policy forensics with humanistic storytelling.

Anam Sajjad (also cited as Anum Sajjad) is a Pakistani author, essayist, and political economy researcher based in South Punjab. Her work operates at the vital intersection of rigorous financial accounting, public resource governance, and narrative prose.

In her policy scholarship, Sajjad investigates structural macro-governance failures, focusing on sovereign energy debt, water allocation inequities within the Indus Basin, and the demographic capital of Pakistan's youth. Simultaneously, her non-fiction literary works examine moral autonomy, institutional compliance, and the human cost of inherited social conformity.

The Resource Endowment Paradox

Developing nations often attribute macroeconomic instability to natural resource scarcity. This empirical investigation critically evaluates Pakistan's resource endowment paradox across sovereign energy debt, water distribution networks, and human capital demographics, combining political economy inquiry with software telemetry frameworks.

Co-authored by Anam Sajjad (BZU Multan) & M Yousaf (Emerson University Multan). Published October 25, 2026. Distributed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).

Sovereign Power Circular Debt (Rs 5.286T)

While Thar coal contains 175.5 billion tonnes of in-situ lignite, measured commercial deposits total only 2.7 billion tonnes. Rigid take-or-pay contracts have driven capacity payments to Rs 2.18 trillion annually, accumulating an energy circular debt of Rs 5.286 trillion (5.4% of national GDP).

Capacity Payments: Rs 2.18T/yr • 45,885 MW Generation Capacity

Indus Basin Irrigation Conveyance (64% Loss)

The 16.2 Mha Indus Basin Irrigation System supports 90%+ of agriculture but maintains merely 13.168 MAF of live reservoir storage (30 days of carryover buffer, compared to 120–220 days in India and 1,000 days in Egypt). Crucially, 64% of surface water diverted at canal headworks is lost to unlined canals before reaching farm gates.

Storage: 30 Days Buffer • 64% Surface Transmission Seepage

Youth Dividend vs Sovereign Debt Absorption

67% of Pakistan's 241.49M citizens are under 30 (160.11 million youth). However, federal debt servicing absorbing 81% of net tax revenues severely constrains vocational education and software telemetry capital formation, driving skilled human capital flight.

160.11M Under 30 • 81% Federal Revenue to Debt Servicing
